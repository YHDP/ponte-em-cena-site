The three workshops

You will spend most of the session on your feet. It is not a lecture, not a class, and you do not need to know how to act. The body goes first; the talking comes after, and it is sharper for it.

What taking part is like

The room has chairs that move, a table for cutting, and one empty wall. Nobody memorises a script and nobody gets on a stage. What is asked of you is a response: an image made with your body, a clipping from a magazine, one sentence at the end.

One rule organises everything, and it is the opposite of what school teaches: body first, language after. Whoever starts from the analytical frame has already decided what they will find. That is why the reading in colours, the most conceptual part, only arrives in the last twenty-five minutes.

We work with groups that already exist, and taking part costs nothing.

What happens

Frozen sculptures, in silence, made from today's news. The group builds one, looks at it, and sets it in motion once.

What you do

Step into an image with your own body. No speaking, no explaining.

What comes out

The room loses its formality before any discussion starts, which is the only way the discussion afterwards is worth anything.

What happens

The two news reports are read alternately, sentence by sentence. The Curinga stops at the most charged moments and asks what the room saw.

What you do

Answer with posture, not with words. A sentence grates, the body reacts, and the reaction is visible to everyone.

What comes out

It becomes obvious that the two texts chose different characters for the same story.

What happens

In subgroups of four to six, a collage answers one question: where does this story land in our lives? Then everyone walks the walls, ninety seconds at each of the other collages.

What you do

Cut, glue and choose. Interpreting while you make is not allowed, and that ban is the method: you talk only once it is done.

What comes out

The workshop's material. The collages stay on the wall and feed the reading in colours later.

What happens

Collages always throw up big vague words: trust, extraction, belonging. Here they get a body. The Curinga goes first, to show that nobody has to be an actor.

What you do

Pick one of those words and turn it into a physical image.

What comes out

Words that would have slipped past become concrete things you can disagree about.

What happens

The five colours of Kleurendenken arrive, taught on the spot in ten minutes. Each colour is a way change happens.

What you do

Answer three questions per scene: which colour is the protagonist using, which one is the journalist using, and which appears nowhere at all. Then finish the fixed sentence: this situation looks [colour], but it needs [colour].

What comes out

The diagnosis. And it is almost always in the absent colour, not the dominant one.

What happens

In groups, four prompts: what has already been tried, which two things both need to be true, who the actors are and which of them is the most invisible.

What you do

Write the paradox as one sentence: we need X, but X prevents Y.

What comes out

The raw material of the scene, and the formulation that jams it.

What happens

Each group casts a protagonist, an antagonist force, and a setting that makes the paradox unavoidable. Target: three to four minutes of scene.

What you do

Write the opening line. That is the line that gets rewritten at the end.

What comes out

A short scene that does not resolve, which is precisely the point.

What happens

The scene runs once, whole. Then it runs again and the audience interrupts: four to six interventions per scene.

What you do

Step into the protagonist's place and try another route. Or stay seated and watch who tries.

What comes out

One capture line per intervention, in the fixed grammar: tried [action] by appealing to [value, actor or lever].

What happens

Every strip goes to the wall. No commentary yet.

What you do

Read what the whole room tried, all at once.

What comes out

The field made visible: everything that was tried, side by side.

What happens

The lines are clustered by what they appeal to. Three to five clusters, each named in one word.

What you do

Cluster them, and disagree with how the others clustered theirs.

What comes out

Step 5 of Frame Creation, done with what the room produced rather than with a ready-made list.

What happens

The decisive question: what did no intervention appeal to? Out of the absences comes a new frame, in the form what if this isn't a problem about X, but about Y?

What you do

Rewrite the scene's opening line under the new frame.

What comes out

The test: if the scene now wants different characters, the frame really moved.

No clock for this one yet

The first two workshops have a settled design. The third does not, and that is deliberate: the format is in co-design with the Consulate-General, and whoever arrives chooses which Dutch method the workshop on deciding will work with.

What is already decided is the Brazilian side, Legislative Theatre, and the rule of the room: authority enters as a horizontal participant, and what the group produces can be submitted somewhere. The rest gets written together.

When the design settles, it appears here in the same shape as the other two.

What comes out of a workshop

Every session generates output, not only input. A Ponte em Cena workshop ends with material: a crossed reading of how the same story lives differently in the two countries, a set of collages mapping where it lands in the lives of the people in the room, and a diagnosis naming which mode of change is in play and which is absent.

In the third workshop the ambition is larger: a proposal that can be submitted, and that neither side would have written alone.

What theatre brings

Body, presence, experience, conflict and participation. The tacit layer interviews never reach.

What design brings

Listening, structure, analysis, collaborative making. Language for what the body revealed.

And the subject?

Not settled yet, and that is deliberate. The approved proposal provides that the theme of each workshop is defined together with the Consulate, and names three possibilities: cultural communication, water and resilience, and two hundred years forward.

The choice changes the material that enters the room. It does not change the clock, or the pair of methods.
