Programme 2026

The three workshops

Diagnose, generate, decide. Three formats under test, each crossing a technique of Augusto Boal with a Dutch design method. The subject of each cycle is chosen together with the Consulate; the movement does not change with it.

The arc

The three workshops are deliberately different formats. The first brings the Dutch context into a Brazilian room without requiring Dutch people to be there. The second puts both sides around the deadlock. The third opens the table and asks who decides.

The pilot cycle began in 2026. Workshops run with groups that already exist, in partnership with the organisations hosting them.

Diagnose →

Newspaper Theatre × Context Mapping + Kleurendenken. In pilot.

Two news reports on the same subject, one from each country, read in parallel. The group answers with the body before it answers with words. No Dutch people needed in the room: the Netherlands arrives through the newspaper.

On the page: a worked example of a news pair, the five Newspaper Theatre questions, the Dutch layer and the selection criteria.

Forum Theatre × Frame Creation. Designed, looking for a host.

A deadlock played out and an audience allowed to interrupt. Every intervention shows which lever somebody reached for, and the question that does the work comes afterwards: which one did nobody reach for?

On the page: the full mechanics, the reframing step and the kind of host we are looking for.

Decide

Legislative Theatre × Dutch layer in co-design with the Consulate.

The workshop that closes the cycle asks who decides, and rehearses the answer rather than debating it. Authority enters as a horizontal participant, and what the group produces can actually be submitted somewhere.

It has no page of its own yet because it is not settled yet. The content is the Consul's choice, within the theme.

What comes out of a workshop

Every session generates output, not only input. A Ponte em Cena workshop ends with material: a crossed reading of how the same story lives differently in the two countries, a set of collages mapping where it lands in the lives of the people in the room, a diagnosis naming which mode of change is in play and which is absent.

In the third workshop the ambition is larger: a proposal that can be submitted, and that neither side would have written alone.

What theatre brings

Body, presence, experience, conflict and participation. The tacit layer interviews never reach.

What design brings

Listening, structure, analysis, collaborative making. Language for what the body revealed.

And the subject?

Not settled yet, and that is deliberate. The approved proposal provides that the theme of each workshop is defined together with the Consulate, and names three possibilities: cultural communication, water and resilience, and two hundred years forward.

The choice changes the material that enters the room. It does not change the movement or the pair of methods, which is what these three pages describe.
