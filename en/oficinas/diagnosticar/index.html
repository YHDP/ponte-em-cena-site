Workshop 01 · diagnose

Reading the other

Two news reports on the same subject, one from each country, read in parallel. The group answers with the body before it answers with words, and only then names what each framing left out.

In pilot

How it works

This is the workshop with the lightest logistics of the three: it does not need Dutch people in the room. The Netherlands arrives through the newspaper. That is why it opens the cycle, and why it runs first with a Brazilian group of young people.

The arc runs from the body to language, never the other way round. It starts with image warm-ups, moves through the crossed reading of the two reports with a physical, wordless response, opens into subgroup collage about where that story lands in the lives of the people present, and closes with a reading in colours: which route to change the journalism is using, and which one is missing.

Newspaper Theatre is the Brazilian technique; Context Mapping and Kleurendenken are the Dutch layer that comes in after the exercise.

What is it about?

That depends on the cycle. The subject of each workshop is set together with the Consulate before the session, and the approved proposal foresees three possibilities: cultural communication, water and resilience, and 200 years forward.

The method does not change with the subject. What changes is the pair of news reports, which is why it is worth showing how one pair works in practice.

A pair of news reports, by way of example

August 2026 offered a pair that is almost too good, within the water and resilience hypothesis. Both countries facing the same phenomenon, in opposite phases, telling the story in ways that look nothing alike.

How the Dutch press told it

The Rhine at its lowest since 1880. Convoys cutting loads by up to 80%, the port of Duisburg running at a third, freight rates more than doubling, surcharges per container.

Vocabulary: cost, capacity, industrial output, supply chain.

How the Brazilian press told it

Amazon rivers recovering after two historic droughts. Early dredging on the Madeira waterway, a monitoring dashboard, preparation for the next dry season.

Vocabulary: supply, isolated communities, food, fuel, medicine.

It is the same subject. River drops, transport stops. But on one side the story is about what it costs, and on the other about who goes without. Neither text is wrong; each one decided who the character is.

With that pair on the table, the five Newspaper Theatre questions stop being a school exercise:

  • Who appears in this narrative?
  • Who was not heard?
  • Which interests are present?
  • Which facts were given prominence?
  • What other readings would be possible?
Why this pair

A pair works when the Netherlands shows up plural, disagreeing with itself, rather than as a bloc. If the Dutch material has economists, hauliers and climate scientists at odds, the group cannot reduce the country to a character trait. That is the antidote to an essentialist reading, and it lives in the choice of the news, not in the Curinga's speech.

To situate a pair like this in Brazil, we keep a map of all 5,570 municipalities with four climate and water measurements. Supporting material, not a fixed part of the workshop.

The Dutch layer, after the exercise

Theatre produces the material. The two Dutch tools are there to read what it produced, and they always come afterwards, never before: whoever starts from the analytical frame has already decided what they will find.

Then comes the reading in colours. Each colour names a way change happens: by negotiating interests, by power and rule, by incentive and result, by learning, by people's own energy.

Applied to the pair above, the question sharpens: the Rhine reporting treats the problem as one of cost and incentive; the Amazon reporting treats it as one of infrastructure and rule. Neither treats it as learning, and almost neither as the energy of the affected people themselves. The absent colour is the diagnosis.

How the news is chosen

Selection goes through the Consulate before each session, and follows fixed criteria: apolitical and non-divisive, current, known on both sides, of real interest to someone between 16 and 22, and suitable for minors.

There is also a list of what we avoid, because these are precisely the clichés that jam the conversation instead of opening it: drugs, euthanasia, sex work and immigration. The Netherlands is more interesting than the caricature that circulates of it.

What the group takes away

A crossed reading of how the same story lives differently in the two countries. A set of collages mapping where it lands in the lives of the people who were in the room. And a diagnosis in colours: which mode of change the journalism is reaching for, and which one is missing.

That feeds the next workshop, where the material stops being reading and becomes a scene.
